# Worked example: a frame-locked remake prompt (remake mode)

This is the depth a remake brief must reach: every cut frame, every arc, every word's width and position, a motion law per shot, gotchas, and a stills gate before the full render. The words, wordmark and product line are placeholders (Lorem/Ipsum).

```
<inputs> Ask me for: three words for the headline build (one per act), the product line for the first end card, a wordmark name, a simple mark (or draw one), a palette mood for the horizon, a source of real photographs of round objects, and the music. If I skip any, use these defaults: words "Lorem's" → "ipsum to" → "dolorem"; product line "Lorem 5.5"; wordmark "Ipsum"; mark = a circle ring with its lower half filled solid; horizon = warm amber dusk over a black planet; photographs = CC0 museum objects from the Cleveland Museum of Art open-access API (plates, coins, mirrors, shields, roundels, stained glass, carved discs); music = the reference track. </inputs>

<direction> A 20 second 16:9 brand film, 1920x1080, 25 fps, frames f1–f500. Concept: the edge of the world. Every shot is the curved top edge of a real round object filling the bottom of frame like a planet's horizon, and the cut lands on a new object every 5–31 frames. Motion law: every shot is a STILL photograph with at most a tiny camera drift. All energy comes from the cuts. No in-shot animation except where listed. The word on the arc never animates; it only changes size and texture at each cut. Look: macro still-life photography: real light, real wear, shallow focus where stated, film grain on every frame (monochrome luminance, alive every frame). Type: one classic serif (Georgia regular) for every word, white or ink per shot, sitting just above the arc. Banned: crossfades, eased moves between shots, procedural noise or sine-wave "textures", rainbow or spectral gradients, purple, violet or magenta anywhere, recolouring the reference's own images, any real brand's mark or words, Math.random. </direction>

<build> 1. One renderer that draws each frame as a pure function of the frame number: seed(shot start) for every random value, no timers. 2. Plates: download museum print JPEGs, model the grey sweep with a 2-D polynomial fitted to the border, threshold, keep the single connected object, close and open with 9 px, choke 3 px, feather 1.8 px. Fit a circle to the top rim. Pad each cutout 900 px with edge-extend so no photo border ever reaches the frame. Never apply a contrast curve to the photographs: museum JPEGs posterize. 3. Cache each still shot's plate once and add per-frame grain on top, so nothing freezes. 4. Before rendering, scan the shot table for one-frame outliers and any bad first or last value. Block the render if any exist. 5. Encode H.264 yuv420p, 25 fps, CRF 15, mux the reference audio. Count the frames: 500. Also build a synced side-by-side (REF | REMAKE). </build>

<gotchas> - Fitted arcs fail on blurry, dark or line-drawing shots. Hand-set those with the three-point circle. - Upscaling a museum photo past ~1.8× looks mushy at 1080p. Pick a bigger object instead. - PIL floodfill silently does nothing on a numpy-wrapped image; .copy() it first. - Particles must sit behind the object and the word, never on top. - A bright rim crossing the word kills legibility. Keep every rim below the baseline. - The reference shot S3 looks like a pan but is static. Measure before you animate. - Run a motion critic and a design critic (separate agents, default stance reject) at full resolution. The design critic judges "as expensive as the reference, frame vs frame". Loop until both score 8 or more. </gotchas>
```
